This page describes what Codrlabs Open is, how people take part in it, and the
terms their work happens under. It exists so that nobody has to guess.

:::caution[Plain description, not a contract]
This page explains how things actually work here in plain language. It is not a
contract, not a licence, and not legal advice. Where a signed agreement exists —
a school placement agreement, a repository licence — **that document governs**,
not this page.
:::

## What Codrlabs Open is

Codrlabs Open is the open-source side of Codrlabs, a company based in Canada. It
is a set of public software projects and the documentation you are reading.

It is deliberately separate from Codrlabs client work. No project here is billed
to a customer, and no project here has a delivery date set by one. The
repositories live under
[github.com/codrlabs](https://github.com/codrlabs).

**These projects have no real user base.** Codrlabs' own products do — real
users, real constraints, real reviews — and mentoring here deliberately
simulates those conditions, so contributors can practise against them without
anyone depending on the result. The code, the tooling and the review of your
pull requests are genuine; the users, constraints and reviews around them are
modelled on Codrlabs' production work.

## How people take part

There are three distinct ways, and they carry different expectations.

### 1. Open contribution

Anyone can open an issue or a pull request on a public repository. No agreement,
no onboarding, no commitment on either side. Your pull request gets reviewed;
that is the whole relationship.

### 2. Mentored contribution

Some contributors work with a maintainer over a longer stretch — regular review,
direction on what to pick up, explanation of the reasoning. This is informal and
either side can stop at any time.

### 3. School placement

Where a student's institution recognises the work for academic credit, that is
arranged **directly between Codrlabs and the school**, in writing, before the
placement starts. The school defines what counts: required hours, learning
objectives, evaluation, and what evidence it needs from Codrlabs. Codrlabs
provides supervision and whatever assessment the school asks for.

## What mentoring is not

Stated plainly, because ambiguity here is how people get hurt:

- **It is not employment.** No wage, no salary, no stipend, no contractor
  agreement. Contributors are not Codrlabs employees, workers, or contractors,
  and this is not a paid internship.
- **It is not production work.** Nothing built here ships to the users of
  Codrlabs' products. The users and conditions contributors work against are
  simulated.
- **It is not a job offer or a pathway to one.** Nobody is being evaluated for a
  role, and taking part creates no expectation of future work.
- **It is not exchange of labour for a service.** Nobody pays Codrlabs for
  mentoring, and Codrlabs does not sell it.
- **It has no service level.** Reviews come when maintainers have time, because
  the maintainers are unpaid here too.

Where academic credit is involved, the credit comes from the student's school
under the school's own rules — not from Codrlabs.

## Your work, and who owns it

Contributions go into public repositories.

- **You keep the copyright in what you write.** Codrlabs does not ask
  contributors to assign copyright.
- **Your contribution is licensed under the licence of the repository you
  contribute to.** Check the `LICENSE` file in that repository before you start;
  if a repository has no licence file, ask before contributing, because without
  one your rights and everyone else's are unclear.
- **Attribution is the git history.** Your commits carry your name, permanently
  and publicly.

:::note[Being fixed]
Not every repository has a licence file yet, and the org does not yet publish a
contributor guide or a code of conduct. Those gaps are known and are being
closed. Until a repository has a `LICENSE`, treat it as source-available rather
than open source.
:::

## Privacy

This site is a set of static pages.

- It sets no cookies and runs no analytics or tracking scripts.
- Fonts, styles and search are served from this site itself — no third-party
  requests, so no third party learns you visited.
- Search runs entirely in your browser; queries are never sent anywhere.
- The host keeps ordinary server request logs, as any web host does.

Taking part in a project means using GitHub, which is a separate service with
its own terms and privacy policy. Anything you post there is public.
